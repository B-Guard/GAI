Let's break down what happens when the advantage is positive versus when it's negative. Remember, a positive advantage means that the action we took was good and we want to reinforce it. When the ratio R is higher than one, it means the new probability assigned to the action is higher than the older probability, so the gradient is moving in the right direction or the direction that agrees with the reward. Conversely, a negative advantage means that the action we took was bad and we want to penalize it. When the ratio is lower than one, the new assigned probability is lower than the older probability, so the direction of the gradient would indeed penalize the action. These regions where the advantage and ratio agree with each other are the regions we do want to clip to protect against overzealous updates that might be reading too much into the specifics of the trajectory that we're currently training on. On the other hand, there will be cases where the ratio disagrees with the advantage on whether the action should be reinforced or penalized. This can happen due to noise or imperfect estimation.
